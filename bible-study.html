Bible Study Preparation & Teaching Guide

Follow these steps as you move from studying Scripture to preparing your Bible study.

1. Pray and Begin With the Right Purpose

Ask God for wisdom and seek to faithfully understand His Word.

2. Choose Your Topic or Passage

Clearly identify what you are going to study.

3. Read the Scripture Carefully

Read the passage several times before drawing conclusions.

4. Study the Context

Consider the surrounding verses, chapter, book, and biblical setting.

5. Find the Main Biblical Truth

Identify what the passage is actually teaching.

6. Study Important Hebrew or Greek Words

Use original-language tools when they help clarify meaning.

7. Use the Bible Dictionary

Research important people, places, words, and concepts.

8. Use the Concordance

Find other passages that use important biblical words.

9. Compare Bible Translations

Compare translations while keeping the original context in view.

10. Read the Commentaries

Use commentaries as study helps, not replacements for Scripture.

11. Develop the Main Teaching Point

State the central truth of your Bible study clearly.

12. Build Supporting Points

Organize supporting truths directly from Scripture.

13. Explain the Scripture

Explain what the passage says and means in context.

14. Give Practical Application

Show how the biblical truth should affect everyday life.

15. Write the Introduction

Help the learner understand why the study matters.

16. Write the Conclusion

Bring the main biblical truth together clearly.

17. Put the Bible Study Together

Organize your material into a clear teaching flow.

18. Review for Biblical Accuracy and Clarity

Make sure your teaching is faithful to Scripture and easy to understand.

Questions to Keep Beside You

Use these questions throughout your study.

1. What does Scripture say?
2. What does Scripture mean in context?
3. What biblical truth should people understand?
4. How should this truth change our lives?
5. Can I support my teaching with Scripture?
6. Am I explaining Scripture or merely giving my opinion?
7. Is the message clear enough for someone studying this passage for the first time?

Bible Study Template

Use this structure to organize your study before writing it.

1. Bible Study Title

What is the title of your study?

2. Main Scripture

What is the primary passage you will study?

3. Bible Study Topic

What biblical topic are you teaching?

4. Study Purpose

What do you want the learner to understand?

5. Main Biblical Truth

What is the central truth of the study?

6. Introduction

Introduce the topic and explain why it matters.

7. Scripture Reading

Read and present the main biblical passage.

8. Context

Explain the historical and biblical context.

9. Observation

What do you observe in the passage?

10. Meaning

What does the passage mean?

11. Teaching Point 1

Explain the first major truth from Scripture.

12. Teaching Point 2

Explain the second major truth from Scripture.

13. Teaching Point 3

Explain the third major truth from Scripture.

14. Additional Scriptures

List other passages that support the main teaching.

15. Illustrations and Examples

Give helpful examples that clarify the biblical truth.

16. Practical Application

How should this truth be applied?

17. Discussion Questions

What questions can help people think through the Scripture?

18. Conclusion and Closing Prayer

Summarize the truth and close the study in prayer.
