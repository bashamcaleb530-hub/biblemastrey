📝 Bible Study Template

Use this template as a simple framework for preparing and teaching a Bible study.

1. Bible Study Title

Give your study a clear title that tells people what they will learn.

2. Main Scripture

Choose the primary passage of Scripture that your study will focus on.

3. Bible Study Purpose

Explain why you are teaching this study and what you want people to understand.

4. Main Biblical Truth

State the central truth that comes from the Scripture.

5. Introduction

Introduce the subject and connect it to the lives of the people studying God's Word.

6. Read the Scripture

Read the main passage carefully before explaining it.

7. Context

Explain the historical, cultural, literary, and biblical context of the passage.

8. What Does Scripture Say?

Observe what is actually written in the passage before deciding what it means.

9. What Does Scripture Mean?

Explain the meaning of the passage using Scripture and its context.

10. Bible Study Point 1

Explain the first major truth from the passage.

11. Bible Study Point 2

Explain the second major truth from the passage.

12. Bible Study Point 3

Explain the third major truth from the passage.

13. Supporting Scriptures

Add other Bible passages that support the biblical truth being taught.

14. Discussion Questions

Create questions that encourage people to examine and understand Scripture for themselves.

15. Practical Application

Explain how the biblical truth should affect everyday life.

16. Conclusion

Bring the study back to the main biblical truth.

17. Response / Next Step

Give people a clear way to respond to what they have learned.

18. Closing Prayer

Close by praying about the truth that was studied.

📘 Bible Study Preparation & Teaching Guide

Follow these steps to prepare a Bible study that explains Scripture clearly and helps people understand and apply God's Word.

1. Pray and Begin With the Right Purpose

Begin by praying and asking God for wisdom and understanding. The goal is to faithfully understand and teach Scripture, not simply to express personal opinions.

2. Choose the Bible Study Topic or Passage

Choose a biblical topic or passage that you want to study. Make sure the subject is clear enough to develop into a complete study.

3. Read the Passage Carefully

Read the passage several times. Look for repeated words, commands, promises, warnings, contrasts, people, events, and important ideas.

4. Study the Context

Examine what comes before and after the passage. Consider the author, audience, historical setting, purpose of the book, and surrounding chapters.

5. Find the Main Biblical Truth

Ask yourself: What is the main truth this passage teaches? Build the entire Bible study around what Scripture actually says.

6. Study Important Hebrew or Greek Words

When an important word needs additional study, use Strong's and other language tools to understand the original word and how it is used in Scripture.

7. Use the Bible Dictionary

Look up important people, places, subjects, customs, and biblical terms that help explain the passage.

8. Use the Concordance

Search important words throughout Scripture. This can help you see how the Bible uses the same word or idea in other passages.

9. Compare Bible Translations

Compare several translations to help identify wording differences and gain a clearer understanding of the passage.

10. Read the Commentaries

Use commentaries as study aids. Compare what trusted commentators say while keeping Scripture as the foundation of the study.

11. Develop the Main Bible Study Point

Reduce the central biblical truth to a clear statement that people can understand and remember.

12. Build Supporting Points

Divide the study into logical sections that help explain the main truth without losing focus.

13. Explain the Scripture

Spend most of the study explaining what the Bible says and means. Let Scripture provide the foundation for your teaching.

14. Ask Good Discussion Questions

Create questions that help people observe the passage, understand its meaning, and think about what it teaches.

15. Give Practical Application

Explain how the biblical truth should affect the believer's thoughts, choices, relationships, faith, and daily life.

16. Write the Conclusion

Summarize the most important truths from the study. Bring the teaching back to the main Scripture and main truth.

17. Give a Clear Next Step

Ask what the people studying the Bible should do with what they have learned. Encourage a biblical response.

18. Review for Biblical Accuracy and Clarity

Before teaching, review the entire study. Make sure your points are supported by Scripture, your explanations fit the context, and the study is understandable.

🔎 Keep These Questions Beside You

These questions can help keep your Bible study focused on Scripture.

  1. What does Scripture actually say?
  2. What does this passage mean in its context?
  3. What is the main biblical truth?
  4. What other Scriptures support this truth?
  5. What should people understand from this passage?
  6. How should this truth change our lives?
  7. Am I explaining Scripture or merely giving my opinion?
  8. Can I support what I am teaching with Scripture?
  9. Are my discussion questions helping people examine the Bible?
  10. Is the study clear enough for someone hearing it for the first time?
